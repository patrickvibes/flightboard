<p>The quickest way to put Flight Board on your board. No Arduino IDE, no
downloads — just Chrome or Edge on a computer.</p>

<h2 id="steps">Steps</h2>
<ol>
<li>Plug the board <strong>directly</strong> into your computer with a USB <strong>data</strong> cable
(not a hub — and not a charge-only cable).</li>
<li>Click <strong>Connect</strong> above and choose the port that appears when you plug the
board in (it may be called "USB Serial" or <code>cu.usbserial-…</code>).</li>
<li>Click <strong>Install Flight Board</strong>.
<ul>
<li><strong>First install on this board:</strong> tick <strong>Erase device</strong>.</li>
<li><strong>Updating:</strong> leave <strong>Erase device unticked</strong> — your Wi-Fi, location and
settings are kept.</li>
</ul>
</li>
<li>Wait for it to finish (2–3 minutes), then close the dialog.</li>
<li>Unplug and replug the board (or press its <strong>EN / RST</strong> button). You'll see
the boot animation.</li>
</ol>
<p>New board? Carry on with <a href="/flightboard/first-setup/">First-time setup</a>.</p>
<h2 id="if-it-doesnt-connect">If it doesn't connect</h2>
<ul>
<li>Try another USB cable — many are charge-only.</li>
<li>Hold the board's <strong>BOOT</strong> button while you click <strong>Connect</strong>, then let go.</li>
<li>On Windows you may need the <strong>CH340</strong> USB driver from the board maker.</li>
<li>Still stuck? Use the <a href="/flightboard/installation/">Arduino IDE method</a> instead.</li>
</ul>
