c:T1802,<h1 id="how-the-game-scores">How the game scores</h1>
<p><strong>Game mode is coming soon.</strong> This page explains how it will work. Your
board keeps doing everything it does today; game mode adds a weekly
competition on top, against friends in a league or everyone on the world
table.</p>
<h2 id="every-week-is-a-new-round">Every week is a new round</h2>
<p>Rounds run <strong>Monday 00:00 to Sunday 23:59, Dublin time</strong>. Your board
watches the sky all week and keeps four records. When the round ends, the
week is sealed, trophies are handed out, and everyone starts again from zero
on Monday.</p>
<p>You don't need to do anything during the week. Just leave the board running.</p>
<h2 id="the-four-records">The four records</h2>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Record</th>
<th>What counts</th>
<th>What helps</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td><strong>Fastest</strong></td>
<td>The highest ground speed of any aircraft your board saw, boosted by how close it came (see the rings below)</td>
<td>Jet-stream nights, and planes passing close by</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Highest</strong></td>
<td>The greatest altitude of any aircraft your board saw, boosted the same way</td>
<td>Business jets often fly higher than airliners</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Most tracked</strong></td>
<td>How many different flights (callsigns) your board logged this week</td>
<td>Keeping your board switched on: uptime matters more than location</td>
</tr>
<tr>

<p>Each record shows the flight that earned it, for example
<em>"1,084 km/h ×5 · UAE41 · Boston → Dubai · Wed 21:14"</em>.</p>
<h2 id="the-target-rings">The target rings</h2>
<p>Your board's patch of sky is a target: four rings around your location, out
to <strong>60 km</strong>. Distances are measured along the ground, so a plane counts as
"overhead" when it's directly above you at any height.</p>
<table>
<thead>
<tr>
<th>Ring</th>
<th>Distance from your board</th>
<th>Boost</th>
</tr>
</thead>
<tbody>
<tr>
<td>Outer</td>
<td>30 to 60 km</td>
<td>×1</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Inner</td>
<td>15 to 30 km</td>
<td>×2</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td>Centre</td>
<td>3 to 15 km</td>
<td>×3</td>
</tr>
<tr>
<td><strong>Overhead</strong></td>
<td>within 3 km</td>
<td><strong>×5</strong></td>
</tr>
</tbody>
</table>
<p>Each aircraft gets the boost of the <strong>closest ring it reached</strong>, and only
that one: a plane that flies right over you is ×5, not 1 + 2 + 3 + 5.</p>
<p>The boost applies to <strong>Fastest</strong> and <strong>Highest</strong>. So a jet doing 1,000 km/h
straight over your house (1,000 × 5) beats one doing 1,100 km/h at the edge
of your window (1,100 × 1). Tables always show the real speed or altitude
with its boost next to it, never a made-up number.</p>
<p><strong>Most tracked</strong> and <strong>Busiest sky</strong> have no boost: they reward a board
that's always on and a busy patch of sky.</p>
<p>When a plane crosses the overhead ring, your board flashes <strong>+5 OVERHEAD</strong>
and plays its chime, as it does now.</p>
<h3 id="planes-between-checks">Planes between checks</h3>
<p>Your board checks for aircraft about once a minute, and a jet can travel
13 km in that time. So the game looks at the <strong>path</strong> a plane flew between
two checks, not just the two dots: if the line between them passes over you,
it counts as overhead. If a plane disappears for more than a few minutes,
the game doesn't guess where it went.</p>
<h3 id="planes-that-hang-around">Planes that hang around</h3>
<p>A plane circling or holding nearby counts <strong>once</strong>. It can only count again
after it has been outside your window for 30 minutes.</p>
<h2 id="league-tables">League tables</h2>
<p>In a league, everyone is ranked in each of the four records. Your <strong>league
position</strong> adds up your four places: for example 1st + 3rd + 2nd + 4th = 10.
<strong>The lowest total wins.</strong> If two players tie, the one who tracked more
flights goes ahead.</p>
<p>At the end of the week, the leader in each record wins that week's
<strong>trophy</strong> for the league. Trophies stay in your trophy cabinet for good.</p>
<h2 id="the-world-table">The world table</h2>
<p>Every paired board is also on the world table, ranked in each record. On
the world table, player callsigns are hidden (you appear as something like
<em>ANON · 7Q3K</em>) and only your country is shown, never your location.</p>
<h2 id="fair-play">Fair play</h2>
<ul>
<li><strong>Same rules for every board.</strong> At the start of each round your board
fetches the round's rules (window size, ring sizes, how often to check)
from the game server. The rules are signed, so editing the board's
software changes nothing.</li>
<li><strong>Checked against our own data.</strong> Your board still does the tracking and
sends its records. The server checks them against its own copy of the
same OpenSky data for your patch of sky. Results that match show as
<strong>Matched</strong>. Anything that doesn't is <strong>In review</strong>: it stays on the
table, but can't win a trophy until it's cleared.</li>
<li><strong>Sealed weeks.</strong> Each finished week is sealed with a fingerprint that is
chained to the week before, so past results can't be quietly changed.</li>
</ul>
<h2 id="questions">Questions</h2>
<p><strong>Does living under a flight path win everything?</strong> It helps with Busiest
sky, but not much else. Fastest and Highest reward planes that come close,
and Most tracked rewards keeping the board on. A board in a quiet spot can
still take trophies.</p>
<p><strong>What if my board is offline for a while?</strong> It simply doesn't see planes
while it's off. Most tracked is the record that suffers most. Nothing is
lost from the rest of the week.</p>
<p><strong>Do I need an OpenSky account?</strong> Yes, for game mode. It lets your board
check often enough for a fair game. See
